Republic of Fun — Studio Identity

Client Republic of Fun (Creative Studio)
Pillar Graphic

30s Summary

  • Challenge: Create a studio identity for Republic of Fun Ltd. (ROFL) that looks like a playful revolution: loud, anti-mediocrity and pro-play, but still sharp enough to sit alongside serious partners and clients.
  • Approach: Started with an imagetic logo — joystick and flag — to mark the day the Republic "flew its flag" and declared a gaming revolution. As the studio's voice matured, the identity evolved into a slicker typographic mark using the acronym ROFL, set in cutout, paper-like letters, with grunge/punk textures, vibrant CRT-inspired colours, ink splatters and the "Registered Fun" F mark as a deliberate twist on corporate symbols.
  • Outcome: A flexible identity system that feels like a manifesto in visual form: instantly rebellious, proudly indie, rooted in game culture and unmistakably about one thing — keeping games democratic, creative and always fun.

Studio Identity

Republic of Fun Ltd. (ROFL) was born from a manifesto: refuse boredom, reject mediocrity, and defend the right to play. The studio identity had to look like that manifesto feels — part declaration, part street poster, part loading screen — while still working on decks, storefronts and splash screens. It needed to say "we're serious about games" and "we refuse to take ourselves too seriously" at the same time.

Logo Evolution

The first logo for ROFL was imagetic: a joystick raised under a flag, marking the moment the Republic of Fun "flew its flag" and kicked off the gaming revolution. It looked like a symbol you could march behind — almost like a coat of arms for players.

As the studio's personality developed and the work became more diverse, the need for a slicker, more compact mark led to a typographic version: the acronym ROFL. The letters are treated like cutout paper shapes, a nod to DIY posters, protest graphics and zines. This isn't a corporate logo; it's a badge. The cutout style visually encodes the studio's revolutionary wish to keep the games industry democratic, messy in the right ways, and always fun.

Visual Language & Symbolism

The visual system leans heavily on grunge and punk elements layered over vibrant colours. The palette recalls the brightness of old TV tubes and early videogames — that slightly over-saturated glow you used to see on CRT screens. It's a reminder that games are both pop culture and comfort.

Ink splatters and rough textures suggest first strokes of paint on a canvas, reinforcing the idea that indie games are art. These details keep the identity grounded in the reality of an independent game dev studio: experimental, self-initiated, a little chaotic by design.

One of the key marks is the "F" used where you would normally expect an ®. Instead of "Registered Trademark", ROFL claims "Registered Fun". It's a small act of visual transgression that sums up the brand attitude: we know the rules of commercial branding — and we're here to bend them in favour of play.
